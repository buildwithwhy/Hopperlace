01 / Three kinds of project

Start from the question you need answered.

Each project is hands-on. Prototyping and selected implementation are part of the work wherever they answer the question better than a document can.

A / Choose an AI approach

“There are several providers, tools and ways to build this. We need to know which will work for us, what it will cost, and what we’d be committing to.”

You receive

A comparison of the shortlisted options on your own cases, a recommended approach and system design, and integration prototypes where agreed.

The work can include

  1. 1Define the task, constraints and what a useful result looks like for your users.
  2. 2Shortlist providers, models and tools, and weigh building, buying or combining them.
  3. 3Outline candidate system designs: where information comes from, where people review, and what depends on what.
  4. 4Test the shortlisted options in practice, on cases that reflect real use, more than once.
  5. 5Build integration prototypes where a comparison on paper can’t answer the question.
  6. 6Recommend an approach, with the trade-offs that could change the recommendation.

Each option is compared on

Functionality
Whether it does the task, on your cases.
Cost
Usage and licensing, plus retries and review time.
Effort
Setup, integration and upkeep for your team.
Failures
How it goes wrong, how often, and how visibly.
Dependencies
Lock-in, data handling, and what moving away would take.

How we treat evidence

We distinguish published terms and documentation, provider claims, our own test results, and the conclusions we draw from them. Recommendations make clear what the evidence supports, what remains uncertain, and which trade-offs could change the decision.

  1. 01Published terms and documentationPricing, limits and policies as the provider publishes them.
  2. 02Provider claimsWhat providers say about capability and accuracy.
  3. 03Our test resultsWhat we observed running each option on your cases.
  4. 04Our conclusionsWhat we infer from the evidence, marked as judgment.

B / Develop and test an AI idea

“We see an opportunity for AI in our product or workflow, but need to work out what it should do and whether it can deliver.”

You receive

A working prototype, findings from testing, and a practical recommendation for what to develop next.

The work can include

  1. 1Understand the user’s task and how it is done today.
  2. 2Define what a useful result would look like.
  3. 3Explore suitable tools, models and system designs, including whether to build or buy.
  4. 4Build a focused, working prototype.
  5. 5Test it on cases that reflect real use.
  6. 6Explain what works, what remains uncertain, and the next development step.

C / Improve an existing AI experience

“The demo looks promising, and the dashboard looks fine, but real users still struggle, correct the output, or redo the work.”

You receive

A diagnosis supported by real examples, a check on whether your metrics reflect useful outcomes, tested or implemented changes where agreed, and evidence of their effect.

The work can include

  1. 1Review real examples and where users run into trouble.
  2. 2Check whether current metrics represent useful outcomes, including failures they don’t show and work quietly passed back to users.
  3. 3Agree what success should mean for the people using it.
  4. 4Trace the problem across model behavior, the information the system has, the workflow and the interface.
  5. 5Prototype or implement selected improvements.
  6. 6Compare results before and after on the same set of cases.
  7. 7Hand over test cases your team can rerun, and clear next steps.

03 / What a project can look like

Illustrative example · not a client project

A team wants to help customers turn a collection of documents into an actionable plan.

  1. 1

    Understand the task

    Which documents customers start with, what the plan needs to help them decide, and what a good plan looks like to them.

  2. 2

    Prototype the experience

    A working version that reads a realistic set of documents and drafts a plan customers can review and edit.

  3. 3

    Test the plans

    Run it on representative document sets and check each plan: is it accurate, complete, and something a customer could act on?

  4. 4

    Find the review points

    Where plans tend to need correcting, and where a person should check before anything is acted on.

  5. 5

    Recommend the next step

    What to build next, what to change, and which questions to answer before offering it more widely.

05 / How projects work

Start with one provider decision, prototype or workflow.

i.

Agreed before we start

Scope, inputs, deliverables, timing and fee are agreed before work begins, around the question the project needs to answer.

ii.

Hands-on work

Research, prototyping, testing and selected implementation, in whatever mix the question calls for.

iii.

A clear handoff

Prototypes, tests and findings are handed over so your team can carry on. Projects can stand alone or be followed by a small advisory engagement.

What the work draws on

Customer and workflow discovery · AI product and interaction design · provider, tool, model and build-versus-buy decisions · system architecture, including where people stay involved · functional prototyping and selected implementation · designing tests and interpreting the results
