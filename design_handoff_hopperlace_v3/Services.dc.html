How we treat evidence4 kinds ↓

We distinguish published terms and documentation, provider claims, our own test results, and the conclusions we draw from them. Recommendations make clear what the evidence supports, what remains uncertain, and which trade-offs could change the decision.

Published terms and documentation
Pricing, limits and policies as the provider publishes them.
Provider claims
What providers say about capability and accuracy.
Our test results
What we observed running each option on your cases.
Our conclusions
What we infer from the evidence, marked as judgment.

04 / Evaluation

Measure whether it works, and whether it matters.

Evaluation covers the whole experience and system, not only the model. It answers two different questions, and a higher task score alone doesn’t establish business impact.

01

Does it do the job reliably?

Whether the system performs its intended tasks well and consistently, across realistic cases and repeated attempts.

Evidence from repeated, representative tests

02

Does that produce the outcome?

Whether those tasks contribute to the outcomes that matter to users and the business.

Evidence from real use

Useful measurement involves

  • Choosing the right metricsMeasures that reflect the intended outcome, agreed before testing.
  • Spotting misleading proxiesNumbers that can improve while users are no better off.
  • Counting checking and correctionThe human time spent verifying, fixing or redoing the output.
  • Interpreting the resultsWhat the evidence supports, and what remains uncertain.

Across the experience and system, we assess

01
Functionality & reliability
Whether it does the task on your cases, how consistently across repeated runs, and where it fails.
02
User effort & expertise
The setup, integration, checking and correction your team would take on.
03
Oversight & control
Whether your team can inspect outputs, approve consequential actions, intervene and recover from errors.
04
Maintainability
How readily it adapts as requirements change, without breaking what already works.
05
Portability & dependencies
Lock-in, data handling, required services, and what switching would involve.
06
Cost
Usage and licensing, retries, and the staff time spent reviewing and correcting.

05 / What a project can look like

Illustrative example · not a client project

A small business wants to turn client briefs and meeting notes into useful project plans.

  1. 01Objective→

    Reduce planning effort while preserving important client requirements.

  2. 02Product / system→
    • ·Drafts a project scope and task plan from the documents.
    • ·Links important details back to their sources.
    • ·Flags missing information.
    • ·A person reviews and approves the plan.
  3. 03Evaluation
    • ·Missed requirements.
    • ·Unsupported commitments.
    • ·Whether people can use the plan.
    • ·Total planning effort, including review and correction, compared with the existing process.

Illustrative product behavior

Each commitment in the draft points to where it came from. Gaps are flagged rather than filled in, and nothing is shared until a person approves it. Those same behaviors are what the evaluation checks.
