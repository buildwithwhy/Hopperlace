02 / Tool testingIn development

What makes an AI tool a good fit?

Our comparisons will examine how well an option works for your task, what it asks of you, and the trade-offs involved in choosing it.

  1. 01

    Functionality & reliability

    Does it accomplish your task to the quality you need? How consistently does it work across different cases and repeated attempts, and where does it fail?

  2. 02

    User effort & expertise

    How much setup, guidance, checking and correction does it require? What can you manage yourself, and where is specialist help needed?

  3. 03

    Oversight & control

    Can you understand and check what it has done, approve consequential actions, intervene when needed, and recover from mistakes?

  4. 04

    Maintainability

    Can you adapt it as your needs change, diagnose problems and make improvements without breaking what already works?

  5. 05

    Portability & dependencies

    What are you committing to? Can you move your data, work or setup elsewhere, and what would switching actually involve?

  6. 06

    Cost

    What does a useful result cost—including subscriptions, usage, retries and the human time involved?

Alongside these practical questions, ValueCompass helps you examine the ownership, relationships and documented practices behind your choices.ValueCompass · available now ↑

How we’ll test

Representative tasks, repeated
Each tool gets comparable tasks that reflect real use, and each task is run more than once.
Independent checks
We check the outputs ourselves rather than relying on what the tool reports.
Successes, failures and repairs
What worked, what broke, and how many attempts it took to fix.
Money and time, kept apart
Monetary cost is recorded separately from the time and human assistance a result required.
Inspectable evidence
Example outputs and the limits of each test are published alongside the advice.

The specific tests depend on the task and the category of tool. The six dimensions are common questions, not an identical checklist or a universal score applied to every product.
